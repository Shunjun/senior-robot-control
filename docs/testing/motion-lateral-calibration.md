# Fortress flat-scene lateral stopping candidate

The default safety profile leaves lateral braking unset. The optional `simulation_motion_flat_lateral.yaml` overlay sets 0.3 m/s² only for the owned normal-friction, level Fortress qualification scene and its tested vehicle geometry. It is not a bound for hardware, other surfaces, or the low-friction wheel-spin scene. Apply an overlay deliberately with the `safety_overlay` launch argument and restart the safety processes; their parameter services are disabled during operation.

The calibration probe applied one simulation-step lateral force at each of ±6,000 N, ±12,000 N, and ±18,000 N with the commanded velocity continuously zero. Ground truth was used only as a test oracle. The six measured peak lateral speeds were respectively +0.06276, -0.04339, +0.18903, -0.22291, +0.37877, and -0.35672 m/s. Their full observed lateral excursions were approximately 0.00133, 0.00133, 0.00549, 0.00547, 0.01243, and 0.01246 m. The final two peaks exceeded the configured 0.3 m/s simulation speed bound; they are stress observations, not evidence that this bound holds under arbitrary external impulses.

The captured truth is sampled at 20 ms. The test checks every sampled post-peak position and the entire measured pulse against the reaction-plus-braking envelope, including an explicit two-sample-period allowance at the sampled peak. That allowance is empirical: it cannot rule out an unseen instantaneous velocity or excursion between samples. The 10 Hz scan matcher reported zero lateral delta for these short impulses, so they do **not** establish detection of transient lateral motion. The model remains dependent on its configured speed and sensing assumptions.

`tests/fixtures/fortress_lateral_impulses.json` contains all 711 sampled truth rows (including all six pulse windows), 115 valid independent-motion intervals, all 331 observed zero commands, and hashes binding the original probe capture, world and xacro. The original capture SHA256 is `6b888279825d712968a00ac0ee9abf2f63d6ccbf3027dd10a898478fcd521abb`. The bounded probe owned process group 667976 and recorded no active owned processes after cleanup. Recheck the recorded envelope with `python -m pytest tests/unit/test_lateral_calibration.py -q`; this replay is not a substitute for running the qualified physical simulator scenario.

The overlay also declares `physical_cap_context: fortress-flat-v1`. The safety gate accepts that context only with the tested footprint, the configured 0.3 m/s linear and 0.5 rad/s yaw caps, reaction time at least 0.2 s, hard margin at least 0.02 m, and no greater braking decelerations than the profile values. Those caps are explicit operating assumptions; the force probe itself shows that sufficiently large external impulses can exceed them. A missing or mismatched context stops motion.

For each valid independent motion interval, the gate validates the full covariance as positive semidefinite and computes an unclipped three-marginal-standard-deviation bound on interval-average SE(2) twist. Marginal bounds do not establish a calibrated joint confidence probability. The interval mean is not used as a limit on instantaneous velocity: the safety worker sweeps the entire assumed physical speed and yaw caps, using both longitudinal and lateral braking distances and a conservative corner-rotation displacement. The same margin applies to a newer-scan obstacle veto. All 711 truth samples and 115 valid independent-motion intervals from the probe are retained for replay; observed interval endpoint residuals fit the reported covariance model in this limited data. Neither this observation nor 20 ms truth sampling proves behavior between samples or on other surfaces.

The separate `fortress-fixed-stand-v1` context belongs only to the test-owned fixed-stand fixture. Its numeric speed, reaction and braking caps are the same conservative model, but the fixture qualifies them through a rigid Gazebo joint that keeps the real chassis fixed while its wheels remain free. This dedicated world has zero gravity and a floor 0.2 m lower, so the robot does not fall during the observed detach-to-attach handshake and the free wheels have no floor contact; these are test-stand conditions, not a mobile-floor braking calibration. Before any drive request the test observes new detached and attached states and checks stationary wheel feedback, absence of a motion anomaly, and physical wheel-to-floor clearance. It retains the full safety and arbitration path and checks actual body and wheel measurements. This stand assumption does not qualify a free low-friction floor or real hardware; the low-friction case remains stopped without its own physical qualification.

Motion progress uses up to 64 independently measured source intervals in original order, with at most eight processed per callback. A source interval retains its original integer endpoint identities and Fusion receipt time; both its ROS age and the original steady pending deadline must remain valid. A received scan superseded for publication is used only for internal progress accounting and is never republished as current Gate evidence. Missing or unqualified continuity stops motion with `progress_history_unqualified`.

The simulation profile declares a 0.001 m/s translation and 0.001 rad/s yaw threshold for a stationary-consistent requalification observation. Hardware defaults are unqualified until separately calibrated. After a real continuity break, the trusted local coordinator may call `/motion_fusion/requalify_progress`; an accepted response only arms the check. New requested-zero and applied-zero streams and two complete, contiguous, independently aligned stationary-consistent scan, wheel and IMU intervals are then required before a fresh source interval can qualify. Ordinary protective or watchdog zeros do not constitute this explicit request. The service neither authorizes remote control nor clears a real slip latch; a fenced real-fault reset remains deferred to 4a. These observations cannot prove motion between samples or qualify uncalibrated hardware.
